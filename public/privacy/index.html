Eye O Ewe

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Eye O Ewe is an app for keeping track of shared expenses and payments in a group. This policy explains what information the app and its service handle, why, who else is involved, how long it’s kept and what your rights are.

Eye O Ewe is run by aleapproved in the United Kingdom (“we”), who is responsible for your information under UK data protection law. You can contact us about anything in this policy at privacy@eyeoewe.app.

In short

  • Your groups are end-to-end encrypted. We can’t read their names, the names of the people in them, or any expense or payment.
  • We don’t use advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting services, and we don’t sell your information.
  • Eye O Ewe keeps a record of who paid what. It never connects to your bank or moves money.

What we can’t see

Group names and icons, the names and icon styles of the people in them, and every expense and payment (amounts, descriptions, categories and how they’re split), along with summaries of imported history, are encrypted on your phone before they reach our servers, and only the people in the group can read them. We can’t read them, and neither can anyone we work with. We never receive your password or recovery words.

What encryption doesn’t hide: which account is in which group and its role there, who added or changed each thing and when, roughly what kind of change it was, and how big it is. Encryption protects your groups from us, not from the people in them: everyone in a group can see its whole history, including what was added before they joined, and anyone who leaves keeps what they could already see.

Other members may add your name, and expenses and payments that involve you, to a group. These are encrypted in the same way.

What we handle, and why

  • Your email address. To create your account, send you a code to confirm your email when you create an account or reset your password, and tell you when your password changes. We don’t send marketing emails.
  • Your password. We never receive it. Your phone turns it into a sign-in credential and an encryption key; our servers store only a scrambled (hashed) form of the credential. The keys that unlock your groups are stored with us only locked by your password or your recovery words. If you save your password or recovery words in a password manager or a file, they’re kept there, under that service’s terms.
  • Account and group records. Random IDs for your account, your groups, their entries and invitations; which groups you’re in and your role; and when each change was made. We need these to run the service. The secret part of an invitation link never reaches our website or servers.
  • Your internet (IP) address. Our servers, and our website’s host, see it when your phone or browser connects. Our sign-in service also records it, with your phone’s app details, for each signed-in session, until you sign out or delete your account.
  • Your phone’s notification token, if you turn notifications on. To send you notifications about your groups. On Android, when someone adds an expense or payment in a group, the notification carries that entry still encrypted, so your phone can open it and show who added what; our servers and the notification services can’t read it. If your phone can’t open it, it shows a general message instead. Once your phone shows the details, apps you have allowed to read your notifications can read them, as with any notification, and they can appear in Android’s notification history. A group’s key doesn’t change when someone leaves, so a former member who somehow obtained such a notification could read that entry.
  • Security and usage limits. Counts of how often an account adds things, and records of wrong password attempts, to stop abuse.
  • Support emails. If you email us, we get your email address and whatever you write. If you choose to attach the app’s diagnostic report, it holds your app version, phone make and model, Android version, text size and time zone, and a list of the app’s recent technical events and error codes with their times. It never holds your groups, names, amounts, recovery words or any of your encrypted information. Your phone keeps this list for up to 7 days, and it’s only sent if you attach it.

You need an email address and a password to use Eye O Ewe; without them we can’t create your account. Everything else is optional. We don’t make decisions about you by automated means.

We don’t collect your contacts, photos, location, microphone, or payment card or bank details.

Our website. eyeoewe.app sets no cookies and loads nothing from other companies. If you choose light or dark, your browser remembers it on your device only. An invitation link keeps its secret after the # in the address, which your browser never sends to us.

Our legal reasons

  • To provide the service you asked for: your account, email address, sign-in, groups, invitations and notifications.
  • Our legitimate interests: keeping the service and your account secure and preventing abuse (usage limits, limiting password guesses, password-change emails), keeping backups, and answering emails you send us. We use as little information as we can for each.
  • Legal obligations: where the law requires us to keep or share information.

Who else is involved

We use these companies to run Eye O Ewe. Each gets only what it needs:

  • Supabase, which hosts our database, sign-in and server functions in London. It holds your account and group records, your encrypted groups, notification tokens and usage counts, and sees your internet address when the app connects.
  • Resend, which sends our emails. It gets your email address and the email’s text.
  • Expo and Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging), which deliver notifications. They get your notification token, random IDs for your account, the group, the entry and the person who added it, a general message such as “There’s new activity in one of your groups.”, and on Android, the new entry still encrypted.
  • Cloudflare, which hosts our website. Like any website host, it sees the internet address of people who visit it.
  • Fastmail, which hosts our inbox. It holds emails you send us.
  • Google Play, which distributes the app. Google handles your Google account and Play activity under its own privacy policy.

Some of this information is handled outside the UK: Supabase handles some account, support and log information in Singapore and the United States, and Resend, Expo, Google, Cloudflare and Fastmail process information in the United States, Australia and elsewhere. Each of these transfers is protected by safeguards UK law accepts: the UK’s approved contract terms for international transfers, or the provider’s certification under the UK–US data bridge. Email us for a copy of these safeguards.

Everything travels over encrypted (HTTPS) connections, and only we can access our service accounts.

How long we keep it

Your account information is kept until you delete your account.

To delete your account, first leave or delete each of your groups. We then delete your email address, password record, profile, encryption keys, pending invitations, notification tokens and recovery information straight away. We keep only your random account ID, as a record that it existed and of which groups it was in and what it added. Expenses, payments and your name as written in groups you were in stay, encrypted, in those groups’ history and on members’ phones.

Your groups, including removed expenses and payments, are kept until an admin deletes the group, or permanently deletes those expenses and payments, which deletes them from our servers straight away. A record of each notification we send you (which group it was about, and when) is kept until you delete your account.

Notification tokens are deleted when you turn notifications off, sign out or delete your account, and stop being used once a phone no longer has the app. Usage counts are short-lived: they’re cleared as newer ones are recorded, normally within a day. If you enter a wrong current password when changing your password, a record of it is kept until you next change your password or delete your account. Records of password-change emails are cleared after 30 days as newer ones are recorded, and when you delete your account. Personal invitations stop working after 7 days; group invitation links work until an admin resets them. Imported history that an admin undoes is deleted after 30 days. We keep support emails for 2 years after the conversation ends.

Our database is backed up every day, and each backup is kept for 7 days. We also keep a copy taken before each update that changes our database, until the next such update replaces it.

Supabase, which runs our sign-in, keeps a log of sign-ins and account changes, which can include your email and internet address; deleting your account doesn’t delete it. It and our other providers also keep their own technical logs, such as internet addresses, for the periods in their own policies.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your information, to correct it, to delete it, to receive it in a form you can reuse, or to restrict how we use it. We can’t read your encrypted groups, so a copy we send includes only what we hold in readable form; your groups’ contents are in the app.

You can object at any time to our use of your information for our legitimate interests.

Email privacy@eyeoewe.app and we’ll reply within one month.

You can delete your account in the app, or without it: see Delete your account.

If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

Eye O Ewe is for people aged 18 and over. If you think a child has given us information, contact us and we’ll delete it.
